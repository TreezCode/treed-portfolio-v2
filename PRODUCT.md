## Primary audience

Hiring managers, recruiters, engineering leaders, and potential collaborators evaluating Joey for software engineer, front-end engineer, product engineer, or full-stack TypeScript roles.

Visitors usually arrive from a resume, LinkedIn, GitHub, an application, or a referral. They skim quickly and need credible evidence of judgment, ownership, engineering craft, and secure-by-design thinking.

Prospective website clients are secondary. They receive one clear route to Codorus Web instead of a competing sales journey inside this portfolio.

## Product purpose

Build With Treez should earn Joey an interview or substantive technical conversation by making four things immediately clear:

1. Joey is a product-minded software engineer.
2. He has shipped production client work, deployed products, and inspectable developer tooling.
3. His information-security education and Security+ foundation shape how he builds.
4. His interface craft includes distinctive, intentional 3D and motion work without sacrificing usability.

The site itself is evidence, but it does not replace clear case-study facts or an accessible resume.

## Positioning

**Category:** Product-minded software engineer

**Differentiator:** Secure-by-design implementation, high-craft front-end work, and end-to-end release ownership.

**Primary action:** Review selected work.

**Secondary action:** Download the resume.

**Closing action:** Contact Joey about an engineering role.

## Portfolio contract

- The hero states the target role and value proposition without waiting for animation.
- Selected work follows immediately and explains the problem, Joey's ownership, important decisions, verified evidence, and current limitations.
- Professional experience includes independent engineering work, credentials, education, and accurate additional employment history.
- GitHub, LinkedIn, resume, email, project status, and employment dates stay consistent.
- Codorus Web is present as a clearly separate path for business website services.
- WebGL is progressive enhancement. Essential identity, content, navigation, and actions remain usable without it.

## Proof and claim boundaries

- Production launches may be described as launches when directly verified.
- Lab scores, tests, security controls, and release checks must be labeled as technical evidence.
- Deployed validation products must not imply adoption, retention, revenue, or market success without data.
- Founder-owned projects demonstrate execution and product judgment, not independent third-party validation.
- Prototype and beta status must remain adjacent to the relevant project.
- Traffic, rankings, conversions, leads, revenue, ROI, awards, and security guarantees are never implied without direct, approved evidence.

## Brand personality

**Precise, dimensional, quietly confident, and human.**

- **Precise:** evidence is explicit, states are labeled, and interface details are deliberate.
- **Dimensional:** sacred geometry, Three.js, and spatial motion are signature expressions rather than decorative wallpaper.
- **Quietly confident:** strong work and honest boundaries replace hype, badge walls, and inflated claims.
- **Human:** the portfolio may show Joey's personality and taste. It should not read like a generic recruiter template or a duplicate of Codorus Web.

## Design principles

1. **Proof before claims.** Every strong statement has nearby evidence or a clear boundary.
2. **Personality without friction.** Sacred geometry and motion remain memorable while navigation and content stay immediately understandable.
3. **3D as a signature.** WebGL creates a few meaningful moments; disciplined 2D surfaces carry the information.
4. **Curation over completeness.** Selected work and a concise working set are stronger than project grids and badge walls.
5. **Security in the architecture.** Privacy, authorization, validation, failure behavior, and release controls are part of the product story.
6. **Never trap the user.** Scroll, keyboard navigation, reduced motion, and mobile composition work without instructions.
7. **One portfolio, one job.** Employment is the primary journey. Business-service demand moves to Codorus Web.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Require meaningful landmarks and names, full keyboard navigation, visible focus, strong contrast, 44 CSS-pixel touch targets, reduced-motion parity, responsive composition, labeled form errors, and useful content when JavaScript, WebGL, animation, or optional media fails.

## Success measures

The primary outcomes are interviews, qualified engineering conversations, useful recruiter feedback, and applications in which the portfolio strengthens Joey's candidacy.

Supporting signals may include resume downloads, case-study engagement, GitHub visits, contact delivery, and recurring recruiter questions. These are diagnostic signals, not employment outcomes by themselves.

## Non-goals

- Turning Build With Treez into a roofing, local-service, or general web-agency marketing site
- Duplicating Codorus Web's service offer or sales funnel
- Publishing fictional businesses as if they were client work
- Presenting a template marketplace, technology badge wall, or generic SaaS-style portfolio
- Hiding core identity or proof behind animation
- Claiming adoption, growth, security, ranking, conversion, or revenue outcomes that are not established
- Removing the sacred-geometry identity merely to resemble conventional portfolios
